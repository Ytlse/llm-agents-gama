\section{Results at the nominal regime}
\label{sec:results}

The nominal regime is an ordinary simulated day, the one the household travel survey
describes, with no disruption in it. Four findings come out of that day.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The fifteen decision-makers separate into five groups. No generative agent reaches the
    four tabular references.
  \item Tuning the general criteria can align some dimensions of the distribution, and
    leave the others where it found them.
  \item A typed classifier that writes no text reaches the same band, for a fiftieth of the cost.
  \item Two decision-makers that the aggregate cannot tell apart disagree on one trip in three.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Fifteen decision-makers on one error scale}
\label{sec:scale}

The fifteen decision-makers spread over the composite axis in five groups that do not
overlap (Figure~\ref{fig:scale}). The composite counts error, so a lower value is a closer
match to the survey distribution. The three floors carry the largest error, from 27.0 for the
minimum-duration heuristic to 50.2 for a uniform draw over the choice set. The four
decision-makers under the minimal prompt land between 7.0 and 14.8. Under the expert prompt,
the three language models land between 4.9 and 9.0. The four tabular references hold inside
half a point, from 3.60 to 4.09.

The minimal prompt already carries less error than the best of the three floors. The worst
of the four minimal-prompt conditions sits at 14.8, against 27.0 for the minimum-duration heuristic,
which picks the fastest option offered. A model that receives the facts of a trip, and no
general criteria, therefore carries behavioural information. That information is not
the one the physical heuristic encodes.

On two versions of one model family, the backbone model weighs as much as the instruction
it carries. Served unchanged to a second model from the same provider, the expert text
leaves 4.15 composite points between the two, interval $+$2.99 to $+$5.43.

Replaying one condition under three seeds moves its composite less than the cohort itself
does. The tuned \texttt{gemini-3.5} condition scores 4.86, 4.65 and 4.30 on the same cohort
and the same evaluation set. The range is 0.56 point, against a cohort resolution of
$\pm$1.3 points. Two further conditions replayed the same way range by 0.05 and 0.24 point.
The sign of their difference holds on all three seeds.

\subsection{What tuning moves, and what it does not}
\label{sec:tuning}

Tuning the general criteria improves every language model, by more than a cohort
would move on its own (Table~\ref{tab:gains}). The paired gain on the composite is 2.27
points for \texttt{gemini-3.5}, interval $+$1.40 to $+$3.22, then 3.37 points for
\texttt{gemini-3.1} and 7.25 for \texttt{mistral-large}. All nine intervals exclude zero. The
gains run from one and a half to five and a half times the cohort
resolution.

These gains come from one tuned text, and its route is not the only one. Each mutation
moves several strata at once, in both directions. The procedure keeps only those whose
over-represented mode recedes.

This text creates a slope rather than a level shift (Figure~\ref{fig:distance}). Under one
kilometre the car share of \texttt{gemini-3.5} does not move, 9.7\,\% before tuning and
10.1\,\% after. Between one and fifty kilometres it rises by six to seven points in every
band. The tuned agent
then carries less error along distance than any tabular reference, 14.2 weighted points
against 16.8 for the random forest. Distance is the only dimension where an agent manages it.

Two errors coexist in the same decision-maker, and the tuning answers only one. The three
language models put the bike share between 6.8 and 8.1\,\%, where the survey
records 4.1\,\%. This text moves it by less than one point, and pulls public transport down
by four to eleven points. One stratum resists
every decision-maker, the 15--19 year olds (Appendix F).

\subsection{Deliberation does not decide}
\label{sec:deliberation}

The typed classifier reaches the band of the four tabular references. Its composite on the
second cohort is \textbf{[c2, out-of-sample composite of the typed classifier]}, against 3.60 to 4.09
points for the four tabular references. The paired differences against those four references
are \textbf{[c2, four paired differences with their 95\,\% intervals]}.

A decision-maker that writes no text scores in the same band as those that write a
justification. What helps is therefore the reading of the trip, not the sentences produced
after the choice. Verbalised deliberation added nothing to aggregate fidelity here. We state
that as a result on this bench, not as a property of language models.

At an equal load of 23{,}026 decisions the classifier costs 1.06 dollar against 49.28, a
factor of about fifty.

\subsection{The aggregate does not tell the individual}
\label{sec:individual}

The tuned agent and the gradient boosted model sit within the cohort resolution of each
other and still disagree on one trip in three. They put their highest probability on a
different mode for 30.3\,\% of trips, counted where both saw at least two options. That
compares the distributions, not the mode each one drew. Between two tabular methods the same
figure runs from 8.4 to 11.0\,\%. Two agents also differ four times more than two tabular
models in the whole distribution, 40.0 points of median distance against 9.8.

On the declared days of the survey the agent designates the declared mode slightly less
often than the tabular references (Table~\ref{tab:unit}). The audit covers 9{,}621 trips of
2{,}930 respondents, each carrying the mode its respondent declared. The tuned agent reaches
67.6\,\% accuracy against 71.5\,\% for the gradient boosted model, a paired gap of 3.87
points, interval $-$5.47 to $-$2.28. Its gap to the multinomial logit separates from zero on
neither accuracy nor cross-entropy.

The typed classifier falls under the all-car floor on individual accuracy. It reaches
64.7\,\% where the floor reaches 66.7\,\%, a paired gap of 2.04 points, interval $-$3.78
to $-$0.29. No paired comparison separates it from the four tabular references on the composite.
The two other aggregate readings put it behind the kernel regression and the random forest.
Its probability mass goes to walking rather than to public transport. Walk recall reaches
56.3\,\%, against about 44\,\% for public transport (Figure~\ref{fig:audit-modes}). The
aggregate split hides that exchange. A population can therefore reproduce the modal split
of a territory while getting more individual trips wrong than a rule that reads nothing.

These four findings hold on the ordinary day the survey describes. A survey tabulates no
other day, and the next section leaves that regime for an event that no variable encodes.
