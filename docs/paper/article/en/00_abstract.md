Urban mobility simulations rely on tabular models or on explicit rules; either way the space of representable behaviours is fixed a priori: a factor encoded as neither variable nor rule cannot influence any decision. LLM agents promise to lift it; we ask two separate questions: can such a population stand in statistically for a territory's, and can it react to contexts no survey tabulates?

We evaluate mode choice in Toulouse against the certified EMC² 2023 survey, under a contract that gives every model the same information: 1,000 sealed eqasim personas, 2,645 trips, OpenTripPlanner itineraries, decisions inside GAMA, and the same 21 variables for the LLM agent, a multinomial logit and a LightGBM oracle fitted on the survey. Four levels are compared — naive floors, bare LLM, a prompt rewritten by hand toward the published shares, tabular references — the agent's probability mass over the modes actually offered being read against the tabular models' renormalised probabilities.

We test one pre-registered hypothesis: neither LLM condition reaches the tabular reference within the equivalence margin — L1 errors of **[xx]** and **[xx]** points against **[xx]** (logit) and **[xx]** (oracle). Two regimes no survey tabulates are then judged on robustness, not fidelity: five-day hysteresis after a metro line failure, with and without decaying memory, and five dated local news events with twenty pre-registered directional predictions, **[xx]** % met.

This argues for hybrid architectures: the tabular model secures calibration, the LLM agent handles departures from the nominal regime.
